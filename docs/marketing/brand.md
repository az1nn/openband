# Brand Foundation

## Brand idea

> **Creative agency.**

OpenBand exists to increase what a musician can do without reducing their control over the work.

That idea should survive a product rename. The identity should feel like a serious creative instrument, not a developer demo, crypto project, AI wrapper, or neon gaming product.

## Brand attributes

1. **Instrument-grade** — precise, responsive, deliberate.
2. **Open** — transparent, extensible, understandable.
3. **Independent** — creator control without anti-establishment posturing.
4. **Immediate** — low friction between idea and sound.
5. **Human** — technology serves the musician; AI is never the protagonist.

## Visual territory

The current product design already provides a usable foundation:

- dark background around `#0A0A0D`;
- dark surfaces around `#141418` and `#1C1C22`;
- current signal/primary red around `#FF3B30`;
- cool blue accent around `#007AFF`;
- neutral system grays;
- mono typography available for technical/data surfaces.

### Recommendation

Keep the **dark studio + signal red + cool blue** family, but give colors clear semantic roles:

- **Near-black** — primary brand canvas; focus and studio atmosphere.
- **Signal red** — record/live/critical action and selected brand moments. Avoid flooding the marketing site with it because red already carries transport meaning.
- **Electric blue** — navigation, links, technical/open-source accents, secondary CTA.
- **Warm white** — primary marketing typography instead of pure white where possible.
- **Neutral gray** — secondary information and chrome.

The product should look like equipment that happens to be software.

## Design principles

### 1. Signal over decoration
Use waveform, meters, transport states, track regions, cables/routing, knobs, timelines, and real project material as graphic language. Avoid generic gradients, floating glass blobs, fake 3D chrome, and meaningless AI particles.

### 2. Real product before illustration
The most persuasive visual asset is an authentic project going from blank studio to export. Product screenshots, close crops, short motion loops, and audio-reactive states should dominate launch materials.

### 3. Dense where musicians expect density
The DAW can be information-rich. Marketing surfaces should create breathing room around that density rather than flattening the product into a generic SaaS aesthetic.

### 4. Red means something
Because `#FF3B30` maps naturally to recording, live state, clipping warnings, and destructive actions, use it intentionally. If every CTA is red, the UI loses semantic precision.

### 5. No fake studio mythology
Avoid clichés such as mixing consoles photographed in dark rooms unless they show an actual user context. The brand should express contemporary music-making, not imitate analog prestige.

## Typography direction

Prioritize open/system fonts and performance.

- Product/UI: continue system UI unless there is a validated reason to change.
- Marketing display/body: Inter, Geist, or another well-supported open sans-serif family.
- Technical labels / metadata / BPM / hashes / code: existing monospace stack or an open mono family.

Do not create identity dependence on a proprietary font before brand-name clearance.

## Image direction

Preferred hierarchy:

1. real OpenBand studio screenshots;
2. short product motion captures;
3. musicians actually recording/playing through the product;
4. detail crops of waveforms, mixer controls, pedalboard, MIDI, and export;
5. restrained diagrams for local-first ownership and open architecture.

Avoid:

- stock "producer with headphones" imagery;
- fake DAW screens;
- AI-generated hands/instruments for product proof;
- abstract generative art as the primary brand identity;
- visual claims for features that are not launch-grade.

## Icon/logo direction

Do not finalize a new logo until the naming gate is resolved.

When the name is clear, evaluate identity concepts against these criteria:

- recognizable at 16–32 px;
- no dependency on a literal musical note;
- works monochrome;
- does not resemble BandLab's mark or the unrelated OpenBand app;
- can encode concepts such as track, signal, branch, open path, or waveform without becoming a diagram;
- works as app icon, favicon, social avatar, and GitHub social preview.

## Naming gate

### Why it is P0

There is an unrelated **OpenBand** already distributed in Google Play and Apple's App Store in the same product territory: musician collaboration, recording, mixing, AI, stems, social features, and publishing. Even if no legal conflict ultimately exists, the market-confusion and discoverability cost is high.

### Gate before final identity

For each candidate name, validate:

1. Google and Bing exact/near-exact results;
2. Apple App Store and Google Play exact/near-exact results;
3. GitHub repository/org names;
4. `.com`, `.app`, `.audio`, `.music`, `.studio`, `.dev` and practical domain options;
5. X, Bluesky, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Reddit and Discord handles where relevant;
6. WIPO Global Brand Database plus target-country trademark databases;
7. music software, DAW, plugin, label, artist, band, and audio-hardware conflicts;
8. phonetic ambiguity in English and Portuguese, then Spanish if LATAM expansion is intended;
9. searchability and spelling after hearing the name once;
10. ability to extend from studio → collaboration → community without becoming misleading.

### Scoring model

| Criterion | Weight |
| --- | ---: |
| Same-category conflict risk | 25 |
| Search ownership potential | 15 |
| Trademark clearance potential | 15 |
| Domain/handle availability | 10 |
| Memorability | 10 |
| Pronunciation EN/PT | 10 |
| Product-fit / extensibility | 10 |
| Visual identity potential | 5 |

Any candidate with a severe same-category conflict is rejected regardless of total score.

### Naming territories to explore

These are **territories, not cleared names**:

- ownership / independence;
- track + branch / versioning;
- session / studio / room;
- signal / wave / frequency;
- forge / foundry / workshop;
- open path / freeform / canvas;
- ensemble / parts / layers.

Do not publish a shortlist until candidates are actually checked.

## Brand architecture

Recommended structure after naming is resolved:

- **Master brand** — the creator-facing studio.
- **Studio** — main creation environment; avoid making it a second brand unless required.
- **Community** — feed/collaboration layer under the master brand.
- **Open-source project** — same master brand, not a hidden developer sub-brand.
- **Hosted services** — if introduced later, use descriptive modifiers rather than fracturing the identity.

## Brand behaviors

The brand earns trust through product behavior:

- a visitor can try before signup;
- failure states are explicit;
- export works;
- local ownership is understandable;
- cloud/AI transitions are consentful and visible;
- roadmap and source are public;
- changelogs admit limitations rather than burying them.

The strongest brand asset at launch is not the logo. It is a trustworthy ten-minute creative session.
