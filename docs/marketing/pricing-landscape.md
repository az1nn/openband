# Pricing Landscape

> Status: dated market intelligence, not OpenBand pricing policy. Re-check live sources before quoting competitor prices externally.

## Purpose

This document tracks **economic models and pricing boundaries** in adjacent music-creation products. The goal is not to undercut competitors mechanically. It is to understand what users are already trained to expect from free, paid, open-source, subscription and institutional offers.

OpenBand's current product contract remains unchanged:

> **Core local creation stays free.**

There is no public OpenBand subscription/pricing commitment yet.

## Snapshot — 2026-09-13

| Product | Free/core boundary | Paid model observed | Strategic lesson |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| BandLab | free creation tier | Pro subscription; Max expansion; promotions and annual discounts | accessible creation can coexist with paid advanced tools/services |
| Soundtrap | free plan can create/save/finish projects | paid personal plans + education subscriptions | usable free core can funnel into deeper content/features and institutional seats |
| Ardour | source code freely available; self-build possible | paid ready-to-run access/support via subscription or one-time payment | open source can monetize convenience, updates and support without owning user projects |
| LMMS | free/open-source product | no core subscription observed | fully free community software remains a credible category reference |

## BandLab

Source of record for this snapshot: [`source-registry.md`](./source-registry.md), S-003 and S-004.

Observed canonical-plan facts at check time:

- Free tier exists.
- Pro: US$14.99/month.
- Pro annual: US$99 first year, then US$149/year.
- Max annual: US$199 first year, then US$299/year.
- Max monthly was marked coming soon.

A separate BandLab promotional landing page displayed a **US$69 first-year Pro offer**.

### Implication

Do not represent BandLab with one permanent price. The useful pattern is:

> **free creation → subscription for advanced creation/artist services/platform benefits → promotional acquisition pricing**

BandLab also bundles value beyond DAW features, including distribution, opportunities, AI tooling and promotion/community perks. Therefore a future OpenBand paid plan should not be benchmarked only on “number of audio features.”

## Soundtrap

Sources: S-006 and S-007.

Observed:

- a free version is positioned as capable of making and finishing real projects;
- premium loops/instruments/features sit behind paid subscriptions;
- current paid personal plan families include Sound Starter and Music Production;
- education uses separate quote/seat economics.

Exact current consumer prices were not recorded in this snapshot because an authoritative static pricing value was not available in the checked support material.

### Implication

The important pricing pattern is:

> **functional free entry → paid depth/content/workflow benefits → separate institutional offer**

This supports OpenBand's decision to avoid making project opening/export a paid dependency.

## Ardour

Sources: S-008 and S-009.

Observed at check time:

- subscription support tiers: US$1, US$4, US$10 and US$50/month;
- source code is available without charge for users willing to build it;
- the FAQ describes a US$45+ one-time ready-to-run payment path with the stated update benefits.

### Implication

Ardour demonstrates a different economic philosophy:

> **software freedom + paid convenience/support/distribution of official builds**

This is strategically relevant because OpenBand also uses open source as an agency/trust benefit. Optional convenience can be monetized without converting local project ownership into a subscription dependency.

## LMMS

Source: S-010.

Observed:

- 100% free;
- open source;
- community-driven.

### Implication

“Open source” does not imply a user expectation of paid hosting/support, nor does it imply zero willingness to pay. OpenBand must earn the right to monetize an incremental service rather than monetizing openness itself.

## Monetization archetypes

### A. Premium creation depth

Examples in category:
- advanced tools;
- larger sound libraries;
- high-quality export options;
- tuning/mastering/AI helpers;
- workflow accelerators.

OpenBand fit: **possible later**, but paid gates must not break the free local creative loop.

### B. Hosted collaboration / sync

Value:
- cross-device state;
- team/project sharing;
- presence/collaboration;
- backup/convenience.

OpenBand fit: **strategically aligned future option** because hosting has real recurring cost while local ownership can remain independent.

### C. Paid compute

Value:
- stem separation;
- expensive inference/DSP;
- optional AI processing;
- rendering services.

OpenBand fit: **strong potential unit-economics model** if local/BYOK alternatives remain clear where feasible.

### D. Distribution / artist services

Value:
- distribution;
- promotion;
- licensing/opportunities;
- career services.

OpenBand fit: **not validated** and far from launch wedge. Avoid copying BandLab's broader ecosystem before core creation/retention works.

### E. Support / managed deployment

Value:
- institutional support;
- managed hosting;
- deployment assistance;
- SLAs;
- training.

OpenBand fit: **possible B2B later**, particularly education/creative organizations, but current readiness is low.

### F. Donations / sponsorship

Value:
- community support for open development;
- low product-policy complexity.

OpenBand fit: **compatible early**, but should not be confused with proof of a scalable business model.

## Pricing principles for OpenBand

Until changed through product strategy:

1. **Opening a locally owned project stays free.**
2. **Exporting the core locally owned project stays free.**
3. **Do not paywall recovery from a user's own local project.**
4. Charge, if ever, for incremental value with a real incremental cost or service benefit.
5. Prefer transparent boundaries: local vs hosted, included vs metered, BYOK vs managed compute.
6. Do not launch pricing before activation and retention are measured.
7. Do not use a competitor's temporary promotional price as the basis for OpenBand economics.

## Future willingness-to-pay research

Do not ask “Would you pay for OpenBand?” in isolation. Use concrete tradeoffs.

Test concepts such as:

- hosted project sync across devices;
- automatic encrypted backup;
- real-time collaboration;
- managed stem separation minutes;
- managed mastering/compute credits;
- team workspace;
- institutional deployment/support.

For each concept capture:

- current workaround;
- frequency of the job;
- current spend;
- pain if unavailable;
- preferred unit (monthly, usage, annual, seat, donation);
- price sensitivity only after value is understood.

## Pricing intelligence rules

- Re-check competitor price within 30 days of use.
- Record country/currency and billing cycle.
- Separate list price from trial, first-year and campaign promotion.
- Separate app-store billing from Web billing when material.
- Never compare a monthly price to an annual effective monthly price without labeling the difference.
- Compare **value boundary**, not only sticker price.
